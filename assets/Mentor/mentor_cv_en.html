My Development as a Mentor

The four MTC badges reflect how I have grown since I started mentoring in May 2026:

  • Vision and Alignment — I connect every session to the purpose of a missionary and to the goals of Missionary Training.
  • Learner-Focused Development — I build trust, set high expectations and keep missionaries actively involved in their own growth.
  • Instructional Excellence — I use explanation, demonstration, guided practice and feedback, and add reinforcement when someone needs it.
  • Resource Proficiency — I base my teaching on Preach My Gospel and the core resources, model how to use them, and help missionaries find answers for themselves.

How I Mentor

  • Trust first. Missionaries feel safe telling me about their real challenges. Two missionaries opened up about worries before an important milestone; we worked through them together and they went forward with the help of local members.
  • Clear expectations and accountability. Goals become daily plans we can measure, and we review them together every week.
  • Start where the missionary is. I learn each missionary's background and adjust pace, language and expectations.
  • Guide with questions. Instead of giving answers, I ask questions that help them discover the principle. After a session on inspired questions, one sister told me it changed her conversations with the people she teaches.
  • Everyone participates. Pair work, role plays and small groups give every missionary a chance to practice before applying it in real situations.

Why Mentor Trainer

As Assistant to the President, I saw how much a mission depends on leaders who know how to train others. As a mentor, I have seen how much a missionary grows when someone sees their potential and helps them reach it. I want to put both together: to help mentors build trust, set clear expectations, use the resources well and keep the Savior at the center, so that the training reaches more missionaries than I could on my own.
